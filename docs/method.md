# The method, frozen on 11 November 2026

Purpose: fix the method and the primary metrics before new data (UNSW 2025, HVAC, clinical sites)
is seen. Those data are the validation set. Nothing below is changed after looking at them. If a
change becomes necessary it is added here as a dated note and stated in the paper.

This file is the English text of the project's freeze record. The clinical site is called Site A: a
private eye medical centre in Türkiye. Until 25 November 2026 a second site, Site B, was planned (see
the note of that date).

## Data preparation

- **Flow type**: transport / role / service port @ peer. Service ports 32768 and above form one class.
- **Role**: in connection logs (Zeek, clinical sites) the side that opens the connection. In flow exports: port fan-out, then a port below 1024, then equal ports, then the recorded initiator, then the lower port.
- **Participation filter**: an inbound service counts on a day only if the device sent data to that peer on that day (in Zeek data: the connection was established).
- **LAN peer identity**: MAC address or device pseudonym. WAN peer according to the level.
- **Primary level of detail**: svc (LAN peer fixed, WAN free). Secondary: port and net.
- **Sampling unit**: a local day on which the device is active. The first and the last, partial, day are dropped.

Levels of detail of the peer label:

| Level | Peer label |
|---|---|
| proto | scope only (wan, lan, mcast, bcast), service port dropped |
| port | scope only |
| svc | LAN peer identity, any WAN host |
| dom | LAN peer identity, WAN registered domain when known |
| net | LAN peer identity, WAN /24 (IPv4) or /48 (IPv6) |
| host | exact peer |

## Estimate and rule

- **Rate estimate**: Q1(T)/T, where Q1(T) is the number of flow types seen on exactly one of the first T days.
- **GT-C**: U(T) = χ²(0.95 quantile, 2(Q1+1) degrees of freedom)/(2T) + the positive part of the mean of the last 14 prediction errors N(t) − Q1(t−1)/(t−1), if at least 3 errors exist. N(t) is the number of new flow types on day t.
- **Rule**: enforce on the first day with T ≥ 7 and U(T) ≤ ε.
- **Primary target**: ε = 0.3 per day. Secondary: 1.0 and 0.1.

## Metrics

- **Primary**: the share of learning episodes in which the alert-day rate over the 14 active days after enforcement exceeds ε. 95 percent interval by cluster bootstrap over devices. An alert day is a day on which the device shows at least one flow type that it has never shown before (wording corrected on 24 November 2026, see the note of that date).
- **Secondary**: the share in which the rate of new flow types exceeds ε, the share of learning episodes that reach enforcement, the median enforcement day, the median calibration ratio per device, the Kaplan-Meier certification curve.
- **Comparison**: fixed 7, 14, 21 and 28 days, the quiet streak (3/ε days without a new type), the discovery rate of the last 14 days.
- **Learning episodes**: day 0 for every device and, if the trace is long enough, a new start every 21 days.

## Expectations written in advance

1. In the control network (HVAC) and the clinical data the calibration ratio is about 1 or below (little drift).
2. The alert-day exceedance share of GT-C stays below 10 percent.
3. Fixed windows are clearly worse at the net level.

If these do not hold, the paper reports them as they are.

## Dated notes

**11 November 2026, validation on UNSW 2025 and HVAC.** Estimate, rule and metrics unchanged. The
data formats required three adaptations: (1) UNSW 2025 has no payload length, so the participation
filter uses a proxy (bytes the device sent within its first 10 packets, for TCP more than 132 bytes).
(2) LAN peer identity is the IP address in UNSW 2025. (3) HVAC lasts 7 days, so a 6-hour unit was
used and that part counts as exploratory. The note recorded at the time that all three expectations
held. The audit of 12 November corrected this for expectation 1 on HVAC (point 3 below).
Expectations 1, 2 and 3 remain open for the clinical data.

**12 November 2026, third audit.** Estimate, rule, primary metric and the three expectations unchanged.

1. *Implementation fix.* In the 12-home data the payload sent by the device is now read relative to the record's `device` field. Before, records opened from outside read the payload of the other side. The definition of the participation filter is the same, the code was made to follow it. Effect on results at most 0.007.
2. *Explicit definition of calibration.* The earlier definition was ambiguous and contained a smoothing term of 0.5. Valid definition: first learning episode of every device, T ≥ 7, ratio = total realised new types / total Q1(T)/T. Two numbers are reported together: the pooled ratio (with a cluster bootstrap interval over devices) and the per-device median over devices with a positive estimate. No smoothing. The number of completely silent devices is stated separately. Expectation 1 is tested with this definition in the clinical data.
3. *Expectation 1 on HVAC corrected.* With the 6-hour unit the pooled ratio is 1.89 (port), 2.47 (svc), 1.08 (net), so it did not hold. With a 1-hour unit it is 0.93 to 1.03. The earlier "median 1.00" was an artefact of the smoothing. Expectations 2 and 3 held on all three validation sets.
4. *Secondary analyses registered before the clinical data.* The primary metric (next 14 active days, alert-day exceedance) stays. In addition: (a) the same metrics with the next 7 active days, (b) a sensitivity analysis with weekdays only, (c) the exceedance of fixed 14 days among the devices GT-C certifies and among those it rejects (discrimination), (d) comparison with a quiet-streak rule tuned to certify the same share as GT-C, (e) calibration and coverage of the alert-day estimate D(T)/T and of the distribution-free bound. D(T) is the number of the first T days that carry a type seen on no other day. These were added after looking at the public data, so they are exploratory there and pre-registered for the clinical data.
5. *The svcx sensitivity level* is for public data only (LAN peers that are not catalogued devices and IPv6 solicited-node groups collapse to one class each). The primary level stays svc. At clinical sites the peer identity is the MAC pseudonym.

**14 November 2026, definitions for clinical sites (before any clinical data).** Estimate, rule, primary metric and the three expectations unchanged. These notes fix how the method is applied on site.

1. *Local day.* The local time of the site (Site A: Europe/Istanbul).
2. *Working days.* Site A works six days a week (Monday to Friday full days, Saturday half day). The definition of an active day is unchanged: a local day on which the device produces traffic. For devices that are always on, Sunday is an active day too. The weekday sensitivity analysis of 12 November is reported with two definitions: Monday to Friday, and Monday to Saturday.
3. *Outage days.* A day on which the recorder was down for more than one hour in total inside working hours is a partial day and is dropped (an extension of the rule for the first and last partial day).
4. *Analysis groups.* The site's IT officer classifies the devices before recording starts.
   - Primary group: agentless and fully visible devices. Agentless: embedded devices (IP phone, printer, storage, recorder, camera, medical device) and medical device PCs on which the institution installs no security software because the vendor manages them. Fully visible: all traffic of the device with other devices passes a mirrored port. A PC connected behind an IP phone, and that phone, count as fully visible.
   - Secondary group: general purpose devices (PC, server, virtual machine).
   - Reported separately: partially visible devices (behind an unmanaged switch). They are evaluated only with the traffic that passes a mirrored port and do not enter the primary result.
   - Expectations 1, 2 and 3 are tested in the primary group. The result over all devices is reported as well.
5. *Device identity.* The pseudonym of the MAC address, produced on the recorder. Virtual machines are separate devices.
6. *Remote peer identity.* The net, dom and host levels need irreversible pseudonyms of the remote /24 network, the domain and the address. They are produced only if the site's written permission covers them explicitly. If it does not, only the proto, port and svc levels are computed at that site, expectation 3 cannot be tested there, and the paper says so.
7. *Recording quality.* For every site the coverage (devices seen / inventory) and the packet loss rate are reported.
8. *Choice of department in a large institution.* Conditions: all ports, or the VLAN, of the selected devices can be mirrored. The IT department can provide the device inventory (type, brand, model). No planned change within 8 weeks. The scope contains both imaging and laboratory devices. Among the options that satisfy these, the one with the most agentless devices is taken. The choice, the departments ruled out and the reasons are written down before recording starts.
9. *Continuous recording and interim data.* At Site A recording runs without a break for up to 16 weeks. The first version of the paper is written with the interim data at the end of week 8 and states the cut-off date. The same analysis is repeated with the full data and both are reported. The method is not changed after looking at the interim data.
10. *Minimum of k days (sensitivity analysis).* The primary rule is unchanged: a flow type enters the allowlist if it was seen (after the participation filter) on at least one day of the learning period. In addition, the allowlist size and the alert-day rate after enforcement are reported when the list is built only from types seen on at least 2 and at least 3 different days. Purpose: to measure the price of protection against one-off or malicious traffic that slipped into the learning period. Exploratory on public data, pre-registered for clinical data.

**14 November 2026, how the recorder implements the definitions above (before any clinical data).**
No parameter changes. These are the operational forms of terms already fixed.

1. *Role.* The originator of the Zeek connection record. Service port: the responder port.
2. *Established.* TCP: the responder answered the handshake (Zeek history contains `h`). Other protocols: at least one packet or byte from the responder.
3. *Protocols without ports* (ICMP, ICMPv6, IGMP and others) are one service per peer, keyed by the IP protocol number, as in the public data.
4. *Two capture ports.* A connection recorded on both ports within 2 seconds with the same addresses and ports is counted once. The copy that names more endpoints by their own MAC address is kept.
5. *Outage measurement.* The recorder writes a capture report every 5 minutes for every capture port. A moment counts as recorded if every port has a report with at least one packet covering it. Working hours are set per site before recording starts (Site A: Monday to Friday 08:00 to 17:00, Saturday 08:00 to 13:30).
6. *Remote peers without permission for pseudonyms.* The remote peer is labelled by a coarse service type only. The svc level does not use it.
7. *Recording limit.* The recorder stops by itself after the permitted number of recording days and drops records of any later day.
8. *Group addresses.* Addresses must not leave the site, so the recorder names well-known multicast groups by a fixed word, gives every other group a keyed pseudonym, and collapses IPv6 solicited-node groups into one class (their addresses embed bits of the target's address). In the public data each group address is its own peer. The difference can only lower the number of flow types at a clinical site and applies to every rule alike.

**19 November 2026, audit of the recorder (before any clinical data).** Estimate, rule, primary
metric and the three expectations unchanged. The audit found cases in which a connection log
misstates which flow types a device has. The operational definitions of 14 November are completed
as follows. The public data are flow exports with roles from the port rules and are not affected.

1. *Connections that stay open.* Zeek writes a connection record when the connection ends and stamps it with its start. A connection that lasts for days would count on its first day only, and not at all if it was open before recording started or is still open when recording stops. The recorder therefore also reports every connection that is still open, once for every hour in which it carried packets, stamped with the time of the last packet. A flow type is present on a day if a connection of that type started on that day or an open connection of that type carried packets on that day. The bytes of the hourly reports are not added. Resolution: a connection shorter than one hour that crosses midnight counts on its first day only.
2. *Silence inside a connection.* By default Zeek forgets a TCP connection after 5 minutes without packets and a UDP flow after 1 minute. When packets resume it starts a new record, and the side that speaks first becomes the originator. The recorder keeps a silent TCP connection for one day (longer than the 2 hour keep-alive default of common operating systems) and a silent UDP flow for 2 minutes (the gap that splits flows in the preparation of the public packet traces).
3. *Handshake not seen.* A TCP record that contains neither the originator's SYN nor the responder's SYN-ACK was picked up in the middle of a conversation (start of the recording, restart of the capture, silence longer than a day). Its originator is only the side that happened to speak first. For such a record the role is decided by the rules for flow exports (port fan-out, then a port below 1024, then equal ports, then the lower port), and the record counts for a device only if that device sent a packet in it other than a reset.
4. *Established, TCP (replaces the TCP part of point 2 of 14 November).* The responder took part if it sent a SYN-ACK, an acknowledgement, data or a FIN (Zeek history letters `h`, `a`, `d`, `f`). A reset alone is a refusal. Before, only the SYN-ACK counted, which dropped the responder of every connection whose handshake was not seen. In an hourly report the responder must also have sent a packet in that hour.
5. *Sensitivity analysis registered for clinical sites.* Every result is also computed with roles from the port rules for every record (the recorded originator only breaks ties). One difference is known in advance: a unicast answer to a multicast or broadcast question is a flow of its own, which the answering device opens towards the asker's client port. The port rules call it a service of the answering device once several askers have been seen.
6. *Duplicates across capture ports* are removed within one hourly batch. A copy that falls into the next batch is kept. This does not change which flow types are present.
7. *Records stamped by an unset clock* (before November 2026 or more than a day ahead of the device clock) are dropped and counted.

Effect on the test capture (CICIoMT2024 benign, 22.1 hours, 27 devices, svc level): 188 pairs of
device and flow type instead of 192. The four that disappeared were artefacts. Two servers appeared
to open connections to a client port (records picked up after a silence in which the server spoke
first). One device was credited with a connection to a remote mail port in which it had sent no
packet. One unanswered keep-alive, sent every two hours, had been cut into separate records, the
last of which carried another MAC address for the same peer IP address. With the default timeouts
202 of 2,979 TCP records in that capture had no handshake.

**19 November 2026, two further reviews of the recorder (before any clinical data).** Estimate,
rule, primary metric and the three expectations unchanged. After the audit the recorder was reviewed
twice more, each time starting from the code and the documents alone, without the audit's notes:
once for what can leave the site, once for what is measured. The second review found cases in
which the chain still misstated which flow types a device has. The definitions for clinical sites
are completed as follows. Every case is a hand-made capture in `recorder/tests/scenarios.py` with
the expected flow types written down beforehand.

1. *Hourly reports on the clock, and a last report (replaces the last two sentences of point 1 of the audit note).* Reports are written at every full hour, not so many hours after the connection began, and one more when a connection ends whose last packet lies in a later clock hour than its start. The packets of one report then lie in one clock hour, and so in one local day wherever local time is a whole number of hours from UTC (both sites). A report counts for a side only if that side sent a packet in that hour. Before, only the responder had to, so a device that was switched off was credited with a day on which only the other side sent keep-alive probes. The sentence on resolution is withdrawn: a connection counts on every day on which it carried packets, also if it is shorter than an hour, ends between two reports, or is silent until just before midnight.
2. *Flows without ports that stay open* (a ping every 30 seconds) are reported like any other. Before, the report was lost whenever the originator had the higher address (the lookup of such a flow in Zeek 8.0 works in one order of the two addresses only).
3. *Packets from the unspecified address.* A DHCP request goes from 0.0.0.0 to the broadcast address whichever device sends it, so the connection log held one record for all devices that asked within two minutes, with the MAC address of the first. Such a record now ends at once and every sender has its own. The public packet traces were reduced with one flow per pair of addresses and ports and have the earlier behaviour: there the broadcast type can be missing for a device on a day. The public results are final and are not recomputed.
4. *Protocols without ports (completes point 3 of 14 November).* They have no roles, so the participation filter, which is defined on inbound services, never applied to them: a device that received a ping and did not answer had a flow type with the sender. At clinical sites such a record counts for the answering side only if that side sent a packet. The public data keep the earlier behaviour. The difference can only lower the number of flow types at a clinical site.
5. *UDP records that the connection log turns around.* If the first packet of a UDP flow comes from a port of a known service to another port, Zeek records the other side as the originator. That side may have sent nothing: the flow is the answer to a question that went to a group address (LLMNR, mDNS) or was not captured. Such a record is treated like a TCP record whose handshake was not seen (point 3 of the audit note): roles from the port rules, and it counts for a device only if the device sent a packet in it. This corrects point 5 of the audit note: an answer from the service port counts as the answering device serving that port to the asker, and the asker has no flow type from it. Only an answer from another port (SSDP, WS-Discovery) is a flow that the answering device opens.
6. *Device identity behind a router (completes point 5 of 14 November).* A frame that reaches the capture port through a router carries the router's MAC address, not the device's. The device is then named by the MAC address that answers for its IP address on the local link, which the recorder learns from ARP and IPv6 neighbour discovery on the mirrored networks. Only a device that no mirrored network shows is named by its IP address. Before, a device in a second mirrored network had two names, one for its own traffic and one for traffic through the router. The router's own address is the router, under the pseudonym of its MAC address.
7. *What is a router.* A MAC address is a router if it fronts addresses of 16 networks outside the site (as before, counted in networks, not addresses), or if it carries packets of two local addresses that the neighbour protocol places at other MAC addresses (new: a router between local networks that leads nowhere else). Routers are not devices of the study.
8. *Addresses outside the local networks are remote* from the first record on. Before, such an address counted as a local device until the MAC address in front of it had been learned as a router, so traffic through a router with few destinations changed its label after days. The exception is an address that the neighbour protocol shows on the local link (a site that uses public addresses inside).
9. *Duplicates across capture ports (corrects point 6 of the audit note).* A copy that falls into the next batch is kept. It carries the same names as the first copy once the neighbour table knows both devices, which it does after their first exchange of ARP. In the first hour of a recording a device behind a router can still appear under its IP address.
10. *Outage rule (point 3 of 14 November).* The analysis program has no default for the working hours. They must be given, or the rule must be switched off by name, and the output says which.
11. *Recording limit (completes point 7 of 14 November).* Three limits, whichever comes first: the permitted number of recording days, 25 hourly batches per permitted day (a limit that a wrong clock cannot stretch), and 365 days from the first recording day.

Limits that remain and are stated in the paper: the same address range used in two VLANs is not
told apart. A device behind a router whose own network is not mirrored is named by its IP address,
and a router that does not announce itself in either way above has to be listed by the site.
Presence is exact to the clock hour.

Effect on the test capture (one pass, primary level): 168 pairs of device and flow type instead of
188. 19 pairs belonged to the router, which had been counted as a device under its IP address. 5
were flow types of devices that had sent no packet (4 ICMP messages that were not answered, 1 DNS
answer whose question is not in the capture). 4 are new: the DHCP broadcast of devices that had been
hidden behind the first sender. 19 more kept their meaning and changed their name, because the
router, and one device behind it, are now named by their MAC addresses.

**19 November 2026, second round of the two reviews (before any clinical data).** Estimate, rule,
primary metric and the three expectations unchanged. Both reviews were repeated on the corrected
recorder, again starting from the code and the documents alone. The review of what is measured
confirmed the hourly reports, the last report, the neighbour table and the learning of routers in
every case it built, and found the cases below. Each is now a hand-made capture in
`recorder/tests/scenarios.py` or a test in `analysis/tests/`.

1. *The finished record of a connection that lasts into a later clock hour (completes point 1 of the note above).* A finished record is stamped with the start of the connection, while its history and its statement that the responder took part cover the whole life of the connection. A device that was switched off when a flow towards it began, and answered from the next day on, was therefore credited with the first day as well (a monitoring ping, a heartbeat datagram, a connection picked up without its handshake). For a connection that ends in a later clock hour than it began, the finished record now counts only for its opener, at the start, and only if the opener is known. Which side sent a packet on which day is taken from the hourly reports. A connection that lies within one clock hour has no reports and counts as before.
2. *A report counts packets (point 1 of the note above).* The packet that moves the clock over a full hour belongs to the next report. Its bytes were still in the earlier one, so a responder whose answer was the first packet after midnight was credited with the day before. A report now counts for a side by packets alone.
3. *ICMP error messages (completes point 4 of the note above).* A device that refuses a datagram, or cannot deliver a packet of somebody else, says so with an ICMP error message (IPv4 types 3, 4, 5, 11 and 12, ICMPv6 types 1 to 4). That is a refusal, as a reset is for TCP, and gives the device that sends it no flow type. Before, the refusing device had a flow type with the sender of the refused packet, so a probe from another device, or a late answer from outside, added a flow type to a device that took part in nothing. The public data keep the earlier behaviour and are not recomputed. The difference can only lower the number of flow types at a clinical site.
4. *Tunnels.* The connection log opens tunnels (IPv4 or IPv6 inside IPv4, GRE, and Teredo, AYIYA, VXLAN, Geneve and GTP inside UDP) and records the connections inside them under the inner addresses. The device that keeps the tunnel then had no record of its own, and the inner addresses could appear as devices. At clinical sites the tunnel is the flow of the device, as in the public data: a protocol without ports towards the other end (point 3 of 14 November), or a UDP flow. What is inside a tunnel is not looked at.
5. *Answers from a service port (corrects the last two sentences of point 5 of the note above).* The connection log turns a UDP record around only if its first packet comes from a port of a service it knows. In the recorder's configuration these are DNS (53), NetBIOS names (137), mDNS (5353), LLMNR (5355), UDP port 443 and six tunnel ports. Only for those ports does an answer count as the answering device serving the port. An answer from any other port (SSDP from port 1900, WS-Discovery from 3702, SNMP from 161 to a broadcast question) is a flow that the answering device opens towards the port of the asker. The sensitivity analysis with the port rules for every record (point 5 of the audit note) measures the difference.
6. *Silent UDP flows on DNS ports (point 2 of the audit note).* The connection log ended flows on the ports of its DNS analyzer (53, 137, 5353, 5355) after 10 seconds of silence. They are now kept for 2 minutes like every UDP flow, as that point says.
7. *Duplicates across capture ports (completes point 4 of 14 November).* Two records are copies of each other only if they also agree in the IP protocol number and, for packets from the unspecified address, in the MAC address of the sender.
8. *Networks of the site outside the private ranges (completes point 8 of the note above).* The exception for addresses that the neighbour protocol shows on the local link covers the mirrored networks only. A host of the institution in another routed network with such addresses is remote, unless that network is listed in the configuration of the recorder. This is done before the device is shipped and needs the site's written permission, because hosts in a listed network are named by tokens of their addresses. The recorder warns when it sees addresses outside the listed networks on the local link.
9. *Device types (registered for clinical sites).* The results of the primary group are also reported by device type of the inventory. One effect is known in advance: an IP phone chooses the UDP ports of every call anew, usually below 32768, so at the port and svc levels a call gives both ends new flow types. The port classes are frozen and stay as they are. The results of the primary group are reported once more without IP phones.

Limits that remain and are stated in the paper, in addition to those above: several devices behind
one MAC address (a small router that translates addresses, a wireless bridge) are one device, and a
device that changes its MAC address is a new device. A responder whose only packet in a connection
falls into the first second after a full hour is also counted in the hour before.

Effect on the test capture (one pass, primary level): 160 pairs of device and flow type instead of
168. The 8 that disappeared were flow types of devices that had only refused a datagram: all 55
ICMPv4 records of the capture are "port unreachable" messages. Nothing else changed.

**21 November 2026, third round of the two reviews (before any clinical data).** Estimate, rule,
primary metric and the three expectations unchanged. Both reviews were repeated on the corrected
recorder, once more starting from the code and the documents alone. The review of what is measured
found the cases below. Each is now a hand-made capture in `recorder/tests/scenarios.py` and a test
in `recorder/tests/test_recorder.py`.

1. *Copies across capture ports: reports of two clock hours (completes point 7 of the note above).* With two or three capture ports, a flow that crosses the switches and ends within two seconds after a full hour produced, on every port, the report about the hour before and the last report so close together that the last report was taken for a copy of the first and replaced it. The flow then had no record of the hour before the full hour: at local midnight, of the day. A report is now compared only with reports of the same clock hour, and a capture port gives one copy of a connection: a second record from a port that has given its copy is a new connection. If the same addresses and ports are used again within the two seconds, a record from another port is the copy of the kept record with the same history and packet counts, or else of the oldest one that has no copy from that port yet.
2. *A record written the other way round by one port (completes point 4 of 14 November).* A capture port that misses the first frame of a UDP exchange records it with the answering device as originator, unless the answer comes from a port of a service the connection log knows (point 5 of the note above). With several ports the complete record of another port stood next to it and both were kept, so the answering device had a flow type towards the port of the asker. Such a record is now a copy: of two records of different ports with the ends exchanged, at most two seconds apart, the one that began earlier is kept. With a single capture port a lost first frame cannot be corrected. This is a stated limit, bounded by the loss rate that is reported for every site (point 7 of 14 November).
3. *A late answer (points 3 and 4 of the audit note).* The recorder copies the history of a connection only if every letter is one the connection log can write, and the letter for a SYN-ACK sent by the originator was missing from its list. A record that begins with a SYN-ACK (a device that answers a connection attempt after the client has given up) was written without history, and the answering device, which took part, had no flow type. The list is now the one of the connection log, and a test compares the two. A record with a letter outside the list is counted and reported. The same letter showed a second case when the numbers of the scan test were checked after the correction: if the capture shows the SYN-ACK before the SYN it answers, the connection log turns the record round when the SYN comes, and the letters it wrote before keep the case of the old direction. The server's SYN-ACK then stood as a packet of the client, and a server that sent nothing else had not taken part. The recorder now writes every letter in the direction of the record.
4. *A group address never sends.* The connection log turns a record around when its first packet leaves a port of a service it knows. For a datagram to a multicast or broadcast address the group then stood as the originator and the record was dropped. The recorder turns it back: the sender has the flow.
5. *Addresses outside the local networks (replaces the exception in point 8 of the two notes above).* An address outside the local networks counted as a device of the site whenever the neighbour table had an entry for it. The table is filled from ARP and neighbour discovery, which any device can send, and it is kept on the key stick. A remote host could thus be given a name of its own, which needs the permission of point 6 of 14 November. Such an address now counts as a device of the site only in a record whose own frame carries the MAC address that answers for the address on the mirrored network, and only if that MAC address is not a router. The device is named by that MAC address. For a device with a private address nothing changes. A device with an address outside the listed networks is recognised wherever its own frames are captured and is remote where its packets have passed a router, as point 8 of the note above already says for routed networks.
6. *When the logs are completed.* The connection log completes its files one minute past the full hour instead of on it. The reports about an hour are written when the clock passes the full hour. They went into the file that had just been opened and stayed in memory for another hour, so a power failure took the reports of two hours with it. The records are the same as before. A report is now anonymised an hour earlier than before, so a peer behind a router whose address has not yet been seen in ARP at that time is named by its address in the report (point 6 of the note on the two further reviews of 19 November), where it was named by its MAC address before.

Limits that remain and are stated in the paper, in addition to those above. A service that answers
from another port than the one it was asked on (TFTP) is two flows: the server has no inbound flow
type for the port it was asked on, and both ends get a flow type with the port the client chose. An
hourly report cannot tell a reset from taking part, because its history is that of the whole
connection: a device whose only packet of a day in an old connection is a reset counts as active in
it on that day. A router that is replaced is a new device with a new name, so every flow to the
router itself has a new peer from that day on, at every device. The date of such a change is noted
and the results are reported with it. Two capture ports see the same frame a little apart in time:
a packet that one port sees just before a full hour and another just after it is counted in both

**24 November 2026, audit of the whole analysis (before any clinical data).** Estimate, rule, primary
metric and the three expectations unchanged. The public-data analysis was run again from the source
files, and an independent review checked the mathematics and the text against the code.

1. **Correction of the implementation: recording days of UNSW 2025.** The archives hold one file per local day, and every file runs up to two minutes past local midnight. Where the next day was not recorded, these two minutes appeared in the analysis as an active day of their own: 3 of 88 days in the first archive and 33 of 273 in the second (12 percent of its device-days). A record is now never placed on a later local day than its file (`adapt_new.py`). The data then have 85 and 240 recording days, the numbers of the archives themselves. The definition of the sampling unit is the same, the code was brought into line with it. Effect: the alert-day exceedance of GT-C in the six cells of UNSW 2025 is 0 to 2 percent (before: 0 to 5), that of fixed 14 days at the net level 45 to 63 percent (before: 45 to 64). Expectations 2 and 3 hold as before. Both sets of tables are kept, and the paper states the correction.
2. **The alert day, stated in full (no change of the metric).** An alert day is a day on which the device shows at least one flow type that it has never shown before, neither in the learning period nor on an earlier day after enforcement. Every new flow type alerts once and is known afterwards. All recorded results were computed this way from the start (`gtlib.series`, column `alertdays`). The sentence under Metrics that called an alert day a day with a flow type "not on the allowlist" was a loose rendering and has been replaced. Under a list that stays as it was on the enforcement day, a new type alerts on every day on which it is used. That stricter count is a different quantity (`fixed_list.py`). Whether it becomes a registered secondary metric is decided separately and will be noted here.
3. **Conventions of the code that the text did not state (no change).** A device enters the analysis with at least 10 full active days. The first and the last active day are dropped per device. Prediction errors are counted from day 3 on. A new learning episode starts every 21 days as long as at least 21 further days remain. An episode counts as enforced only if the rule fires at least 14 active days before the end of the trace (or as many days as the horizon in use): later enforcements cannot be evaluated and count as not enforced. The stored tables also hold the variants `GT point`, `GT-UCB` and `GT-max(conf)`, which are not comparison rules of the paper. For the target 1.0 the alert-day metric cannot fail, because an alert-day rate cannot exceed 1: only the type-rate metric is informative there.
4. **Exchangeability test (descriptive, not part of the rule).** All tests shared one random stream, so the result depended on the order in which the database returned the devices and changed from run to run (by up to 0.03 in the pooled ratios). Every test now has its own stream, and 1000 permutations replace 300 (`run_series3.py`).
5. **Preparation of the UNSW 2016 trace made deterministic.** The address of a device on a day is found by a greedy cover of its flow records. When two candidate addresses covered the same number of records, the choice depended on the order in which the database returned rows. In one run of this audit two of 4,943,928 records (malformed IPv6 frames of two devices) changed sides for that reason, and 57 rows of the 14-day stopping table of that trace moved (levels proto, port and svcx). At the target 0.3 the enforcement shares, the enforcement days and the alert-day exceedance were the same in every cell, and the type-rate exceedance moved by up to 0.017 (GT-C at the port level: 4 or 5 of 59 enforced episodes). At the target 0.1 the median enforcement day of GT-C at the port level was 48 or 44.5. Ties are now broken by the address, then by the device name (`normalize3.py`). With this rule the prepared data equal those behind the tables of 12 November record for record, so no recorded table changes.
6. **Computations that were quoted without a recorded script** now have one: `hvac_cal.py` (explicit calibration of the HVAC trace), `facts.py` (descriptive numbers), `run_extra.sh` and `run_cic.sh` (the exact invocations). The point values of the HVAC calibration are unchanged. Its bootstrap intervals differ by at most 0.04 from those of 12 November, whose random stream was not recorded.
7. **Effect of the correction of 12 November, stated precisely.** "At most 0.007" in point 1 of that note refers to the exceedance shares of the rules the paper reports at the target 0.3 (largest change: fixed 14 days, svc level, type rate, 0.106 to 0.099). Over all cells of the stopping table the largest change is 0.014 (target 0.1, recent rate).
8. **Intervals.** The percentile interval of the cluster bootstrap collapses to a point when no enforced episode exceeds the target. Every row therefore also states the number of devices behind it and the exact upper limit for that number of devices (Clopper-Pearson, upper end of the two-sided 95 percent interval). The bootstrap interval of the primary metric is unchanged.
9. **IoT-ServTrace (exploratory).** The frozen rule was also run on IoT-ServTrace (19 devices over 17 months and 52 devices over 12 months). Only the port level can be built from it, without roles and without the participation filter (`adapt_servtrace.py`). Whether these results are reported in the paper is decided separately.

**25 November 2026, decisions after the audit and analyses registered before the clinical data.** Estimate,
rule, primary metric and the three expectations unchanged. Points 1 to 5 record decisions that the note of
24 November left open. Points 6 to 11 define analyses whose code did not exist yet. Each definition was
written here before the analysis was run on any data. On the public data all of them are exploratory. For
the clinical data they are registered.

1. **One clinical site.** The study has one clinical site, Site A. A second site is not sought. Point 8 of 14 November (choice of department in a large institution) and the words "both sites" in the notes of 19 November no longer apply. Replication across institutions is given up. Point 11 puts a replication within the site in its place. The claims of the paper are limited to agentless devices in one private eye medical centre.
2. **The strict count is a registered secondary metric (settles point 2 of 24 November).** The alert-day rate under a list that is never updated (`fixed_list.py`) is reported next to the primary metric in every stopping table. The primary metric is unchanged.
3. **IoT-ServTrace is reported in the paper (settles point 9 of 24 November),** as a check of the stopping rule over a long horizon, at the port level only.
4. **Daily looks.** The paper claims no confidence level for the enforcement day. It reports the realised error rates of the rule. A bound that is valid at every look is an additional analysis (point 10).
5. **Number of devices and exact upper limit** (point 8 of 24 November) are given in every row of the stopping tables of the paper.
6. **Partial recording days (sensitivity analysis, public data).** The public traces have no record of when the capture was down. On a day on which the capture ran for part of the day only, flow types can be missed and then count as new on a later day. Definition: for every capture (a trace, in the 12-home data a home) and every local day, the coverage is the time between the first and the last flow record of any device of that capture on that day. A day with a coverage of less than 20 hours is a partial recording day. The threshold was set on 24 November, when these days were first counted (`facts.py`). In the sensitivity analysis the partial recording days are removed for every device of the capture, and the frozen procedure then runs unchanged on the remaining days. Scope: the 12 homes, UNSW 2016, both archives of UNSW 2025 and IoT-ServTrace. The HVAC trace (7 days, 6-hour unit) is left out. Reported next to the primary analysis: the number of days removed, and for GT-C, the quiet streak and fixed 14 days at the target 0.3 the share of episodes enforced, the median enforcement day, the two exceedance shares and the strict count, at the port, svc and net levels. At the clinical site the primary analysis already drops outage days, from the recorder's own reports (point 3 of 14 November). There the mirror image is reported: the primary analysis once more with the outage rule switched off.
7. **Weekdays only (operational form of point 4b of 12 November and point 2 of 14 November).** The incidence table is restricted to the local days that fall on the chosen days of the week. The frozen procedure then runs unchanged on the remaining days: T counts retained days, and the horizon is the next 14 retained active days. Two sets: Monday to Friday, and Monday to Saturday. Reported as in point 6, for the same traces. At the clinical site both sets are reported, as fixed on 14 November.
8. **Minimum of k days (operational form of point 10 of 14 November).** For every enforcement of the primary analysis (same episodes and same enforcement day, rules GT-C, quiet streak and fixed 14 days, target 0.3, horizon 14 active days) and for k = 1, 2, 3, the list L_k holds the flow types that the device showed on at least k of the first T days. L_1 is the list of the primary analysis. Reported: the size of L_k and its share of L_1. The alert-day rate after enforcement in two countings: alert once (a day counts if a flow type outside L_k appears for the first time since enforcement, for k = 1 this is the primary metric) and never updated (a day counts if any flow type outside L_k appears, for k = 1 this is the strict count of point 2). The share of enforcements in which each rate exceeds the target. The share of the flow records of those 14 days whose type is outside L_k. The enforcement day is not recomputed: the stopping rule uses Q1 as before. The analysis prices the stricter list and does not propose a rule for it.
9. **Quiet streak tuned to the same share: alert-day version (completes point 4d of 12 November).** The tuning is unchanged: the streak length between 5 and 60 days whose share of enforced episodes is closest to that of GT-C at the target 0.3, the shorter one on a tie. So far the paired cluster bootstrap compared the type-rate exceedance only. It now also compares the alert-day exceedance, the primary metric, on the same bootstrap samples. The streak length is chosen in hindsight on the same data, which favours the quiet streak.
10. **A bound that is valid at every look (additional analysis, point 4).** GT-C compares a nominal 95 percent bound with the target on every day, so the 95 percent does not carry over to the day on which it enforces. Daily version: the 0.95 quantile in the first term of U(T) is replaced by the 1 − α_T quantile, with α_T = 0.05 / ((T − 6)(T − 5)) for T = 7, 8, ... These levels add up to 0.05 over all days. The drift term and the rule (enforce on the first day T ≥ 7 on which the bound is at most ε) are unchanged. Under the nominal model of the bound (Q1(T) Poisson), the probability that the first term lies below the expected rate of new flow types of the next day on any day is then at most 0.05. This holds for the enforcement day too, whatever the stopping rule. The two other gaps noted on 24 November remain (Q1 is not Poisson, and the expected rate differs from the realised rate of 14 days). Weekly version: the same, with looks on days 7, 14, 21, ... only and α = 0.05 / (j(j + 1)) at the j-th look. Known in advance: without any singleton the daily version cannot enforce before day 32 at the target 0.3 and before day 126 at the target 0.1, the weekly version not before day 21 and day 84 (GT-C: day 10 and day 30). Reported for the same episodes next to GT-C, at the targets 0.1, 0.3 and 1.0: the share of episodes enforced, the median enforcement day, the two exceedance shares and the strict count with the number of devices and the exact upper limit, and the delay against GT-C on the episodes that both enforce. GT-C stays the rule of the study.
11. **Replication within the site (Site A).** Recording runs for up to 16 weeks (point 9 of 14 November). Recording days are counted from the first local day on which the recorder records. Part 1 is recording days 1 to C. C is the last complete day of the interim export: it is planned as day 56 (the end of week 8) and stated in the paper. Part 2 is the days after C, up to day 112. Part 2 is analysed as if it were a new recording: every device starts learning on its first active day in part 2. Rule, parameters, targets, metrics, analysis groups, the classification of the devices and the version of the analysis code are those of part 1 (the commit is stated, and an error corrected in between is reported with its effect on both parts). Device pseudonyms are the same in both parts. The analysis of part 1 stays the primary analysis of the study (point 9 of 14 November), part 2 is its replication, and the analysis of the whole recording is reported as before. Nothing is changed after looking at part 1. Reported side by side for the primary group at the svc, port and net levels: the three expectations, and for GT-C, the quiet streak and fixed 14 days the share of episodes enforced, the median enforcement day, the two exceedance shares and the strict count with the number of devices and the exact upper limit, the calibration ratio, and the difference between the parts by a paired cluster bootstrap over the devices present in both. Per device: whether GT-C enforced in the first learning episode of each part (a two-by-two table) and the difference of the enforcement days. An expectation counts as replicated within the site if it holds in both parts, as not supported if it fails in both, and as unstable if the parts disagree. The paper reports whichever it is. In addition, as a description of how a list ages: for every device that GT-C enforced in part 1, the list of that day is kept, and the alert-day rate over part 2 is reported in the two countings of point 8. If part 2 has fewer than 42 recording days (6 weeks), it is not analysed on its own: only the whole recording is reported, and the paper says that the replication could not be done (on the public data 25 to 90 percent of the devices could be evaluated at the svc level with 35 active days, and 47 to 89 percent with 42). Limits: both parts come from the same devices at the same site, so this is a replication in time and not across institutions. Changes at the site between or within the parts (public holidays, replaced equipment) are listed with their dates, and the method is not adapted to them. The procedure is tried on the public traces that are long enough (at least 98 recording days: UNSW 2016, the second archive of UNSW 2025 and IoT-ServTrace), with C = 56. That trial is exploratory.
12. **Preparation recorded.** The invocations that rebuild the source files from their parts, unpack them and bring the two base traces into the common schema are now a script (`run_prep.sh`). Before, they were only described in words.
13. **Deakin D-IoT added (25 November, after points 1 to 12 were recorded and before any of its flow records were analysed).** A further public trace: 24 consumer IoT devices in a university laboratory, 119 daily captures between May 2023 and May 2024 (Pasquini and co-authors, doi:10.26187/deakin.28013234). The frozen procedure and every analysis of points 6 to 11 are applied to it unchanged. Three properties of the data are known in advance and are stated with the results. The published captures of the IoT devices hold only the packets that the devices sent, so a flow type is built from what the device itself sent, and the opener of a connection is known for TCP only (a SYN opens, a SYN-ACK answers). The device identity is the MAC address, and a LAN peer is identified by its IP address unless it is a monitored device. The capture ran in batches with gaps of up to seven months between them, and devices were added over time, so new flow types after a gap are expected. Local days are Australia/Melbourne and are taken from the packet timestamps, not from the file names (`deakin_reduce.py`, `adapt_deakin.py`).
14. **How the trial of point 11 counts days on the public traces (25 November, before the trial was run on any trace).** A public trace has days without records. The two parts are cut by calendar days from the first local day with records (days 1 to 56 and days 57 to 112), as at the site. A trace takes part if it has at least 98 days with records. If its part 2 then holds fewer than 42 days with records, the trace is not analysed, and the tables say so. A part in which no learning episode can be evaluated is reported as such. For the description of how a list ages, the enforcement day of GT-C in the first learning episode of part 1 is used wherever it falls in part 1, also when fewer than 14 active days of part 1 follow it. Known in advance for Deakin D-IoT from the dates of its files: its first 56 calendar days hold about 19 days with records, too few for a learning episode that can be evaluated, so that trace cannot show a replication (`sens_units.py`, `halves.py`).
